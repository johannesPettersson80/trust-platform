# 68. Controllability

You will determine whether one pump can move both tank levels to a chosen nearby state in the local model. You will explain why this does not mean it can maintain two arbitrary level setpoints.

## The problem

The pump enters tank 1 directly, while tank 2 responds through tank 1. We want to know whether changing that single pump over time can influence both level deviations independently. That is the question of **controllability**.

## What we need

We need to determine whether one pump can influence both local tank-state
components independently over time. That question must be kept separate
from whether arbitrary steady level pairs or bounded trajectories are feasible.

## The new idea

**Controllability** is the property that inputs can move a model's state
between arbitrary states over time. Here it concerns the unconstrained local
linear model; physical pump and level limits remain separate. We compare
the direct pump influence with the influence propagated through the dynamics.

Use A=[[-.04,0],[.04,-.04]] s−1 and B=[2,0]T m−2. Form the controllability columns B and AB. B has units m⁻² and AB has units m⁻²/s. Multiply AB by a reference time of 1 s when comparing the directions, so both columns use the same units; this scaling does not change whether they are independent.

$$
\mathcal C=[B,AB],\qquad B=\begin{bmatrix}2\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad
AB=\begin{bmatrix}-.08\\.08\end{bmatrix}.
$$

AB=A B=[−.08,.08]T. Thus [B AB]=[[2,−.08],[0,.08]]. For a two-by-two matrix $\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}$, the determinant is $ad-bc$. It is nonzero exactly when neither column is a multiple of the other. Here $2(.08)-(-.08)(0)=.16$, so the columns are independent. The number of independent columns is called the **rank**; it is two. Geometrically, the first pump effect points upstream and the propagated effect has a downstream component; together they span two local transient directions.

At steady state x'=0, the second balance requires x1=x2. One pump therefore cannot maintain arbitrary unequal levels at equilibrium even though its transient influence is controllable.

## Prediction

Calculate AB and determinant .16. Predict the initial direction for a positive v and explain why a pulse followed by restored nominal flow does not instantly return levels to .25 m.

## Implementation

Open `src/Main.st`. It owns one upstream-pump pulse, clock/bound checks and the separate local predictor retained from Lesson67. There is no downstream pump or actuator-layout selector. `FlowDeviationM3PerS` is the reviewed parameter for the positive and negative cases.

In Control Studio's Tune pane, import `provided-model.json`, choose **Analyze model**, and read **State-space structure**. The tool calculates and displays B, AB and the controllability rank. Check those displayed columns against your hand calculation; do not replace this analysis with rank calculations in ST.

## Experiment in Control Studio

1. Restart at h1=h2=.25,q=.010, state zero, and valid dt=1 s.
2. At **Confirm and start**, run separate full-reset 20 s cases with +.001 and −.001 m³/s pump deviations, then restore q=.010.
3. Observe both levels and propagated direction. On normal completion, allow 100 s recovery before the next reset; nominal flow alone does not erase accumulated level.
4. Stop if a physical level leaves .05–.45 m, applied flow leaves 0–.020 m³/s, data become invalid, or time stops. Command zero pump flow and stop the simulator; gravity outflow continues until the simulation is stopped. Reset before another trial.

## Evidence and decision

Retain the determinant and the transient records. A nonzero determinant supports local transient controllability, not arbitrary steady targets, nonlinear global behavior, or actuator authority outside bounds.

## Conclusion

Controllability describes independent transient directions generated by direct and propagated pump action. Steady cascade balance still requires equal levels.
